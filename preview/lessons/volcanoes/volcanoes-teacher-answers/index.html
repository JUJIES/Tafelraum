YEAR 7 · GEOGRAPHY · LIVING WITH VOLCANOES

Why live so close?

Teacher only · Possible answers; reward evidence and balanced reasoning.

1 · Watch: what does the evidence show?

Possible observation: moving or erupting lava. Very hot lava could burn or bury things in its path. Accept any hazard linked to an observation in the actual footage.
The film does not tell us why a particular family lives nearby, what it earns, or whether it could afford to move. Eruption footage alone cannot establish people’s motives.

Ridgehaven: a fictional village

Ridgehaven stands near an active volcano. Old volcanic material has weathered into fertile soil. The Ali family grows vegetables and earns extra money guiding visitors. Their relatives live nearby. A home elsewhere would cost more than they can afford.

Past lava flows have damaged roads. Volcanic gases can harm health. Scientists monitor the volcano, but cannot promise that the village will always be safe. The family has an evacuation plan.

This invented case is not a description of Kīlauea or the people in the film. Fertile soil develops over time; fresh lava is not farmland.

2 · Investigate: read the fictional case

Work with a partner. Record short phrases from the case.

Two benefits
Fertile soil supports vegetable farming; guiding visitors provides extra income.
One hazard
Lava can damage roads; volcanic gases can harm health. Either is valid.
One limit on their choices
The family cannot afford a more expensive home elsewhere. Relatives nearby also give a reason to stay.

3 · Explain: why might the family stay?

Write three sentences: a benefit, a risk, and a limit on their choices. Use because and however.

Staying may protect the family’s farming income and keep them near relatives. However, lava can damage roads and threaten access. Their limited money makes moving difficult; monitoring and an evacuation plan reduce some risk but do not remove it.

Accept different judgements supported by evidence. Monitoring reduces uncertainty; it cannot guarantee safety.
